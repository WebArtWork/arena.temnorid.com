Game guide · version 0.1.0

How it works

Every rule in Temnorid Arena 0.1, with the real numbers the game uses. When the game changes, this page changes with it.

The match

Version 0.1 has one mode: an endless free-for-all deathmatch. Everyone fights everyone, and there is no time or kill limit yet. You leave when you want to.

  1. In the main menu you type your name and press Play vs bots. Your game becomes the host of a new match.
  2. The first arena loads and 5 bots (Bot 1 … Bot 5) appear at spawn points.
  3. Other players can Join by typing the host's address. They enter the same match as more combatants.
  4. You fight. Each defeat counts as a kill for the attacker and a death for the defeated.
  5. Defeated mages come back after 3 seconds.
  6. Leave match in the pause menu saves your kills and deaths and returns you to the main menu.

The game's long-term plan is round-based team matches, like Counter-Strike, with spells bought between rounds instead of guns. 0.1 is the first playable step towards it.

Your mage

Players and bots are the same kind of combatant, with the same body and the same numbers. Colour tells them apart: you are cyan, other players are gold, bots are red.

Spells

Spells take the place of guns. Every mage carries the same four, in the same slots. A spell can be cast when you are alive, its cooldown is over and you have the mana.

SlotSpellEffectManaCooldown
1FireballA projectile that flies at 32 m/s for up to 60 m and deals 25 damage to the first combatant it touches. It stops at walls and cover.120.5 s
2Frost novaA ring of frost from the caster: 35 damage to every other mage within 6 m, even behind cover.306 s
3Arcane shieldA bubble that absorbs 50 damage for 5 seconds. Whatever is left after 5 s fades away.2512 s
4BlinkTeleports you up to 9 m where you aim. A wall in the way stops you just in front of it.205 s

What this means in a fight

  • Fireball is your main attack. Four hits defeat a mage with no shield; a full mana bar holds eight fireballs.
  • Frost nova is the answer to someone close. A nova plus three fireballs is also exactly 110 damage, enough even through a little shield.
  • Arcane shield is worth two fireballs of health. Cast it when the hits start, not after.
  • Blink gets you out, behind cover or onto the central platform. It costs as much as almost two fireballs, so do not waste it.

Balance lives in one place in the code (SpellBook in temnorid-arena-spells), so these numbers are easy to change between versions.

How a cast works

The host decides what happens, so nobody can fake a hit.

  1. You press cast. Your game sends "cast slot N towards this point" to the host. On PC and phones the aim is the centre of the screen; in VR it is the ray from your casting hand.
  2. The host checks that you are alive, the cooldown is over (with 0.15 s of slack for network delay) and you have the mana. If not, nothing happens.
  3. The host spends the mana, starts the cooldown and runs the spell: it flies the fireball, finds who the nova reaches, gives the shield or moves you for a blink.
  4. Damage goes through the target's shield first, then health. When health reaches 0 the target is defeated.
  5. Every player sees the effect: the flying ball, the frost ring, the bubble, the flash at both ends of a blink, and the mage's cast, hit and death animations.

Projectiles always start at the caster's eyes, also in VR. That keeps VR and flat-screen players equal: a hand can aim, but cannot shoot from around a corner.

Defeat and respawn

  • At 0 health you are defeated. The attacker gets a kill, you get a death, and every player's kill feed shows "attacker defeated you".
  • Your mage plays its death animation and lies where it fell. You cannot move or cast, and your screen shows "Defeated by X, respawning in N" and the scoreboard.
  • After 3 seconds you come back with full health and mana at the spawn point farthest from every living enemy, so nobody appears right in front of someone.
  • Falling out of the arena (below 20 m under the floor) defeats you at once.

Bots

Bots run on the host and use the same spells, mana and cooldowns as players. Every 0.35 s or so (a little random) a bot looks at the fight and picks, in this order:

  1. Shield if it is under half health and was just hit.
  2. Frost nova if its target is closer than about 5 m.
  3. Blink away if it is under 30% health and the target is within 6 m.
  4. Fireball if it can see its target, aimed at the chest with up to 4° of error.

Between casts a bot goes for the nearest living enemy, player or bot. It tries to stay about 11 m away: it closes in from further, backs off when too close, sprints when far, and strafes left and right, changing direction every 1–2.5 s. When it gets stuck on cover it jumps and strafes the other way.

The arena

The first arena is a 40 × 40 m stone hall with 4 m walls. In it:

  • a raised central platform,
  • 8 pillars and 2 broken pillars,
  • 2 low walls and 6 crates to hide behind,
  • braziers in the corners, a banner on each wall, rubble,
  • 8 spawn circles around the edge, all facing the centre.

An arena is data, not code: a Scene JSON file (from the engine's open scene format) lists every piece, its place and its role, such as spawn. The host and every player load the same file, so they always agree on the map. Footstep sounds follow the floor surface.

Screen and menus

In a match

  • Health and mana bars; the health bar shows your shield on top.
  • Spell bar with the selected slot, cooldowns, and spells you cannot afford dimmed.
  • Crosshair in the centre (not in VR, where you aim with your hand).
  • Kill feed with every defeat in the match.
  • Scoreboard (kills and deaths of everyone) while you hold Tab, and while you are defeated.

Menus

  • Main menu: your name, Play vs bots, Join by address, Settings, Quit (iPhone apps do not quit themselves, so not there).
  • Pause (Esc): Resume, Settings, Leave match.
  • Settings: master, effects and music volume, look sensitivity and invert look. In VR instead of the look settings: snap or smooth turning, the comfort vignette, and which hand casts. Settings are saved on your device.

Playing together

  • The player who presses Play vs bots is the host: their game runs the match, the bots and every spell.
  • Others press Join and type the host's address (an IP such as 192.168.1.20). The game uses UDP port 7777; on a home network that just works, across the internet the host has to open that port.
  • A player who joins in the middle of a match sees the current scoreboard straight away.
  • Your own movement is predicted on your device, so it feels instant; the host corrects it if the two disagree. Other mages move smoothly between updates.
  • On iPhone the first launch asks to allow local network access. It is needed to host or join.

From the command line (PC)

TemnoridArena.exe -host hosts, -server runs a match with no local player, -client 1.2.3.4 joins, -port 7777 picks a port and -name Ira sets your name.

Every defeat is also sent as an open, engine-independent message (temnorid-arena.player-defeated), and the match announces itself as mode temnorid-arena.deathmatch on map first-arena. Kill feeds, replays and stats can be read outside the game.

Saved stats

When the game can reach its online service, it signs you in anonymously (no account, no password; the sign-in stays on your device). It keeps your name in your profile and adds each match's kills and deaths, and the number of matches played, to your deathmatch totals when you leave a match. Without a connection the game plays offline as usual.

How it is built

Temnorid Arena is the first game on the IT Kamianets 3D Engine, made in Unity 6. The engine is a set of small packages; the game adds its own rules on top. Each layer only uses the ones below it.

Open formatsScene, asset and network schemas in JSON: maps, models and match messages that do not depend on Unity.
Engine packagesCore, scene loading, assets, input for every device, the character, audio, VR, Firebase and networking.
Character View constructorThe first- and third-person camera and controls, plus VR controls.
Temnorid Arena packagescore (combatants, the match, scoreboard, respawns), spells (the spell book, casting, effects, bots), maps (arenas as Scene JSON), hud (bars, spell bar, kill feed, touch and VR panels), menus (main, pause, settings, sign-in and stats).
The game projectThe Arena scene, prefabs for the player, bots and match, the models, and the build menu for every platform.

The source is on GitHub.

What comes next

  • Game feel: more models and sounds, aim feedback, hit markers, smarter bots that use cover.
  • Matches: time and kill limits with a results screen, teams, then rounds with a spell economy: earn gold each round and buy spells before it starts.
  • More arenas, with map selection.
  • Network: fairer hits under lag, smoother movement, results and replays saved by a trusted server.
  • Sign-in with Google on PC, and store releases.
